## 2. Introduction

*Inkward Bound* is an interactive generative installation working with ink to examine the spatialisation of consciousness. As the temporal becoming of consciousness is taken over by AI and flattened into retrievable coordinates, the visitor moves and waits within that space by hand, and the ink follows — diffused, disturbed, condensed. In the seconds when the system does not obey at once, time regains its thickness, bound inward.

The work treats latent space — the computational space inside a model, real but never visible — as a conceptual field, and lets the pipeline enact what it describes. Footage the artist shot of ink in water is cut into frames, grouped and captioned by state, and trained into a LoRA model. These states are mapped onto a single axis from 0 to 1 in latent space, then pre-baked through multi-seed generation and frame interpolation into a retrievable atlas. A continuous flow becomes a set of indexable positions: the very transformation the work is about, carried out in the pipeline rather than argued in concept.

As the visitor touches the screen the C-value shifts. Under pressure it approaches its target smoothly without ever arriving; on release it recedes at a constant rate, so what appears depends on the input that came before, not on the command of the moment. That lag is not interaction polish; it is the only part of the work that is genuinely temporal.

The work does not decide whether gathering means repair, or whether dispersal means loss. It hands the question back to the body — to the time spent pressing, waiting, letting go.

## 3. Concept: From Temporal Being to Spatial Being

**Entry point | The phenomenon: consciousness leaking outward** — when the temporal becoming of consciousness is taken over by AI.

Consciousness comes into being in time — it has succession, it unfolds, its moments interpenetrate and cannot be counted off one by one. When that coming-into-being is taken over by AI it is spatialised: interpenetration becomes mutual externality. **Things can be counted only because they lie outside one another.** Bergson's diagnosis holds here, but his remedy has no counterpart, because this spatialisation is carried out by a technical system outside consciousness and cannot be undone by reflection. The operation performing it is datafication — a continuous flow cut into elements that can be stored, recombined and retrieved. This is not a first occurrence: writing, print, machinery, recording and the digital are the same operation at different depths. What is particular to AI is that **what it takes is protention, not memory** — it completes what comes next on the visitor's behalf, and the present loses its thickness. This is neither damage nor liberation: datafication is what makes new becoming possible and what thins the present, and those are one event. After the flattening, experience falls into latent space — the computational space that exists inside the model and cannot be seen from outside, where it becomes vectors that lie outside one another: coordinates, positions, addresses at which things can be found. One thing does not fall in: a knowing that holds only while an action is being performed, which has no form in which it could be stored. That is why the work has to be made by a body, and it is not a claim that the body is the remedy.

*The above is the whole chain compressed; the sections below take it apart. The premise — that no outcome is assumed — frames the chain and is listed last without taking a place in its order.*

### 3.1 What consciousness is to begin with

Consciousness comes into being in time — duration, retention, protention.

**▸ Something that has before and after, that unfolds**

*Zhang Xianglong; Bergson (durée); Husserl*

Duration is a qualitative multiplicity: its moments interpenetrate, have no edges, and cannot be counted. Listening to a melody, the note sounding now is the note it is because the notes before it are still at work inside it. Husserl's structure is finer still: every present carries a **retention** of what has just passed and a **protention** towards what comes next — the present has thickness; it is not a point.

*Answers: what it is that is being taken over.*

### 3.2 How the takeover and the transformation happen

Spatialisation — from interpenetration to mutual externality.

**▸ Something with coordinates, with positions, that can be retrieved**

*Bergson*

- **Things can be counted because they lie outside one another.** Counting presupposes juxtaposition: three cups on a table do not enter into each other, which is why they can be counted.
- **Representation necessarily transforms.** To present something is to give it form; form means an edge, an edge means a position, a position means juxtaposition. There is no way around this step.
- **Latent space is mutual externality in its textbook form.** Vectors occupy coordinates, distances can be measured, values can be interpolated. A C-axis from 0 to 1 is the assignment of positions to a process that cannot be decomposed.

**A line that has to hold:**

> Bergson's diagnosis holds; his prescription has nothing to attach to here — spatialisation is carried out by technical systems outside consciousness, and cannot be undone by reflection.

What Bergson confronted was a habit internal to consciousness — something that can be reflected upon and suspended, which is why he could argue for a return to duration. The spatialisation at issue here takes place outside consciousness and does not change when one's attitude changes. **This line is the source of the position of not adjudicating**: not an openness of posture, but the fact that the place from which one might adjudicate no longer exists.

*Answers: how the transformation happens, and why a return cannot be argued for.*

### 3.3 Datafication and the pharmakon

**In one line: a multiplicity that could only be felt has become a multiplicity that can be retrieved.**

*Stiegler (grammatisation, tertiary retention, pharmakon) / Husserl / Derrida, "Plato's Pharmacy" / Plato, Phaedrus / Paglen*

- **This is not an isolated case.** A continuous flow is cut into storable, recombinable, retrievable elements — writing, print, mechanisation, recording, the digital: the same operation pressed forward again at successive levels. *(This line has to stay: the argument about ink only holds if writing is in the sequence.)*
- **What is taken is protention, not memory.** Recommendation, autocompletion and prediction perform "what comes next" on the visitor's behalf. When the next moment is always given in advance, the present loses its thickness.
- **Seeing itself has been datafied.** Images are no longer first of all objects to be looked at; they have become units to be retrieved and processed (Paglen, 2016).
- **Pharmakon.** Remedy and poison are one word, and cannot be prised apart. The datafication of language made philosophy possible and made the decay of memory possible in the same gesture — not as a side effect, but as the same event.

**A line that has to hold:**

> Value-neutrality is built into the pharmakon; it does not need to be declared separately.

*Answers: how old this transformation is, and why it is not simply a loss.*

### 3.4 A knowing that cannot be spatialised

There is a knowing that holds only while the action is taking place, and which stops being itself the moment it leaves the present. It has no storable form, and therefore cannot be datafied.

*Merleau-Ponty; Varela / Thompson / Rosch; Wang Yangming*

- Perception is **not** a representation staged in an inner theatre; it is the interaction of body and world itself.
- The unity of knowing and acting: knowledge does not exist prior to action, and cannot be preserved apart from it.

**A line that has to hold:**

> This is an explanation of a mechanism, not a prescription — it accounts for the necessity of form; it does not claim the body as the cure.

*Answers: why it has to be touch, and why it has to be continuous.*

### Premise | No presupposed outcome

Intra-action keeps the relation between person and system mutually constitutive, rather than one party acting upon another (Barad).

*This frames the whole chain; it does not take a place in the sequence.*

## 4. Metaphor: Why Ink

**The argument in full:** ink is not a metaphor for consciousness. It is the material form of the state before spatialisation, it is at the same time the earliest technology of spatialisation, and it performs the same function in two traditions.

### 4.1 Two states of ink (phenomenological register)

*Bergson — qualitative multiplicity / quantitative multiplicity*

- **Ink in water** — interpenetrating, without edges, impossible to count → the state not yet spatialised
- **Ink on paper** — dried, holding a shape, occupying a position, identifiable → already a product of spatialisation

To choose ink in water is to choose the state that has not yet become an object.

*Holds off: could it be paper, smoke or sand instead.*

### 4.2 Ink and technology share an origin (structural register)

*Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015 / Derrida, Stiegler — pharmakon*

- **Shared origin with diffusion models** — both move from noise towards form; that mathematical structure came out of the study of physical diffusion in the first place, so this is not a coincidence of naming.
- **Ink as the earliest technology of spatialisation** — fixing flowing thought as a visible trace is the first version of storing anything at all.

Ink therefore carries a double identity: it is what things looked like before spatialisation, and it is spatialisation's first instrument.

*Holds off: is this only an analogy of appearance.*

### 4.3 The cultural position of ink (contextual register)

*Wang Yangming, the unity of knowing and acting / Merleau-Ponty, Varela*

Ink is a carrier of writing, memory and trace common to East and West. It is chosen not as a cultural sign but because it performs the same function in both traditions — exactly as the two theoretical lines above say the same thing in two languages. The double belonging of the material and the double belonging of the theory share a structure.

*Holds off: is Chinese philosophy here as cultural decoration.*

## 5. Technical Spatialisation: How the Ink Is Flattened

| Step | Operation | What happens to the ink |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Filming and frame extraction | Continuous footage → discrete frames | The flow is cut into single frames |
| 2 Grouping and captioning | Frames → four state groups + text | States are named as categories |
| 3 LoRA training | Images → weights and features | Ink becomes a distribution of features |
| 4 Latent-space mapping | Features → C-axis 0–1 | Each state acquires a coordinate |
| 5 Multi-seed generation + interpolation | Coordinates → pre-baked atlas | Retrievable; continuity is stitched back on |

> Every step makes the ink more retrievable. The last step attempts to manufacture continuity again — which is precisely what the work is asking about.

**Two arguments usable directly in the viva:**

**Pre-baking is not a compromise; it is the argument.** An atlas means generation has already finished: everything the visitor sees already exists inside a retrievable structure, and nothing is "being generated" in front of them. That is exactly what the work is saying. If the image really were coming into being in time, the work would contradict itself.

**Interpolation is honest.** What SLERP, LERP and frame interpolation do is fill in continuity between two positions that lie outside one another. The smoothness is stitched, not native. The work does not pretend to have achieved continuity; it shows continuity being manufactured.

**Operational images.** The images in the atlas exist first to be retrieved and interpolated, not to be looked at (Parikka). What the visitor sees is a cross-section of that operational structure at one coordinate.

## 6. Structure of the Installation

### Interaction

As the visitor touches the screen, the C-value shifts. Under pressure it approaches its target smoothly without ever arriving; on release it recedes at a constant rate, so what appears depends on the input that came before, not on the command of the moment.

The C-value is not a readout of position but an **integral of the gesture over time**: duration of contact carries seven tenths of the weight (eighteen seconds of holding are required to reach the ceiling), steadiness carries three tenths, and agitation subtracts. There is therefore no way to simply set it to 1 — one can only hold, stay still, and wait. Waiting is written into the formula itself rather than requested of the visitor.

**This is the material realisation of the Husserlian layer:**

- Slow onset (pressing does not produce an immediate response) = protention. You do not know what comes next; you have to wait.
- Slow recession (releasing does not snap back) = retention. What has just passed is still at work.
- The current state determined by prior input = the present has thickness; it is not a point.

The lag is produced in two places at once: the browser computes the C-value, and TouchDesigner shapes it further with an asymmetric lag (0.4 s rise, 2.5 s fall) and a Gaussian filter. The fall is far slower than the rise — **gathering takes more time than dispersing** — and that asymmetry is set deliberately, not inherited as a by-product.

That lag is the only part of the work that is genuinely temporal. The system's not obeying at once is also Barad: the outcome is determined by the ongoing back-and-forth, not by either party alone.

### Two screens

| | Main screen | Second screen |
|---|---|---|
| Content | The ink changing state | Machine-vision readouts, coordinates, parameters |
| Viewpoint | Frontal | Overhead |
| Position | You are inside it | You are above it |
| Corresponds to | Qualitative multiplicity | Quantitative multiplicity |
| | Human seeing | Machine seeing |

*Paglen (2016): the two kinds of seeing coexist, are incommensurable, and neither is a gloss on the other.*
*Parikka: the second screen is the operational layer becoming visible.*

**Not a sequential relation.** Both screens are present at once and equal in standing. If the main screen had to be read before the second, the second would become an instruction manual, and would imply that the data side is the more real one. The visitor moves back and forth between two modes of being on their own — and every shift of the eyes performs an act of spatialisation in person.

### Why touch

The installation does not explain itself: no instructions, no visible slider, no path that runs "understand first, then operate". Understanding can only happen in the doing. The time the visitor spends pressing, waiting and letting go is not a route towards understanding; it is the understanding.
